# Amendment 0002: Ink

**Status:** Proposed against SPEC 0.1.0, as section 35. The locked text is left untouched.
**Part:** `packages/web/ink.css` and `packages/web/src/ink.ts`.
**Proof:** `tests/conformance/ink.test.mjs`, measured from pixels in Chrome, WebKit and Firefox.

---

## Why

Since CSS1 in 1996, the web has placed text by an invisible box. Every line carries the font's
room for accents above the capitals and for descenders below the baseline, plus half the leading
on each side. Padding, margins, centring, left alignment and font size are all measured from that
box. The reader sees only the ink.

The gap between the two changes with every face, size and weight, so every designer corrects by
eye, one pixel nudge at a time. Measured before this amendment, in real engines:

- A control with 12px of padding shows 17 to 19px of air above its capitals and below its
  baseline, in nine common faces.
- The same control's capitals sit up to a pixel off centre at 16px, and the error grows with size.
- At 96px Helvetica Neue an H starts 7.4px inside its box, a T 0.0px and an A 0.6px outside it, so
  headlines set flush left do not line up with each other or with the image above them.
- `font-size: 16px` names the box, not the letters: capitals are 71% of it in Helvetica Neue and
  66% in Times.
- An icon is sized by the box and centred on the lowercase letters, so it never meets the capitals.

Four of these are now fixable in CSS, because `text-box` (Chrome 133, Safari 18.2, Firefox 154)
and `font-size-adjust` reached every engine. The left edge and the icon's stroke are not fixable
in CSS at all. Before writing this we looked for existing work. Capsize trims with pseudo-elements
and per-font numbers. Typeset pulls a fixed list of letters by guessed amounts. Material Symbols
has a weight axis you set by hand. Nothing measures the face, and nothing does all of it.

## 35. Ink

Type is placed and sized by what it draws. One attribute, `data-ink`, on `<html>` or on any
container, turns it on. The stylesheet needs no script. The script adds the left edge, the stem,
and WebKit's sizing.

35.1 A text block's box begins at its first line's capitals and ends at its last line's baseline, so the ink above the capitals and below the baseline is the padding, in every face and size.

35.2 The visible gap between two text blocks is the margin between them.

35.3 Lines inside a block stand line-height apart, baseline to baseline, and a change of face or size inside a line (code, sub, sup) does not move them.

35.4 With `data-ink="ex"`, a block begins at the x-height instead of the capitals.

35.5 A glyph marked `data-ink-glyph` stands on the baseline and reaches the capitals (or the x-height, with `data-ink-glyph="ex"`) of the face it sits in, at any size.

35.6 Media in an inked page leave no gap beneath them, and a text block that holds a control or an unmarked graphic keeps its box, so nothing hangs over its neighbour.

35.7 Below `data-ink-size`, every face matches the x-height (or, with `cap`, the cap height) of the face set there, fallbacks and code included.

35.8 Every line of a heading starts at its ink, whatever its first letter, and an opening quote hangs outside the edge so the letter after it aligns.

35.9 A stroked glyph draws at the stem of the face it sits in, measured from that face at that weight.

35.10 The edge is set without changing the document: no node is added or split, and stopping puts every attribute back as it was.

35.11 Ink weighs nothing: each rule is wrapped in `:where()`, so a site's own rule of any weight wins, and `data-ink="off"` restores the box for an element and its contents.

35.12 Ink is lighter than what it replaces: no node is added for the vertical, and the script measures each face and letter once.

35.13 A list is trimmed as a whole: its items keep their leading between them, baseline to baseline, and only the list's first capitals and last baseline meet its edges.

## How each is met

**The vertical (35.1 to 35.4, 35.6, 35.13).** `text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic` (or `ex`) on
the elements that carry text: p, h1 to h6, blockquote, figcaption, caption, th, td, label, legend,
summary, button, pre, address, and anything marked `data-ink-box`. Lists (ul, ol, dl, menu) are
trimmed as a whole, never item by item. A list's items are its lines, and the space between them is
their leading. Trimming each item collapsed the contents rail of khannaoffice.com into overlapping
lines the first time Ink ran on a real site, which is how this rule was found. Containers are left
alone on purpose, for two reasons. A container's trim reaches into its first child's first line, so
it would overrule the child's own rule. It would also cut any line whose tallest thing is a button
or an image: measured in Chrome, a 40px image alone in a trimmed line overlapped the text above it
by 28px.

A text element that directly holds a control or an unmarked graphic keeps its box. Media are blocks.
Code, kbd, samp, sub and sup take `line-height: 0`, so their own ascent and descent cannot widen a
line. Scripts without capitals (Japanese, Chinese, Korean) trim the leading only, until engines
other than Gecko ship the ideographic edges.

**Size (35.7).** `font-size-adjust: ex-height from-font` (or `cap-height`) where `data-ink-size`
is set. Blink and Gecko resolve `from-font` there and inherit the number. WebKit resolves it again
on every element, so `ink()` states the number where it is set. The CSS `font` shorthand resets
`font-size-adjust` by specification, so faces below are set with `font-family` and `font-size`.

**The left edge (35.8, 35.10).** The script renders each letter once to a canvas and reads its
ink edge from the pixels, refined by the coverage of the first inked column. It does not use
`measureText`'s bounding box, which WebKit reports as 0 and Gecko gets a pixel wrong. Then it moves
the block, without touching the document:

- The whole block moves by `translate`, which reflows nothing.
- The first line moves by `text-indent`.
- Each later line moves by a zero-width `::before` float whose `shape-outside` polygon gives that
  line its own inset.

Engines read a float's shape across a whole line box, and lines touch, so each step in the polygon
sits a quarter of the line pitch inside the narrower of the two bands. A line then only ever
overlaps a band narrower than its own, so each line gets exactly its own width, whatever the
rounding.

Engines also disagree on where the float's top stands. Blink and WebKit put it at the first line
box's top, Gecko at the content edge. The script checks where the lines landed and re-anchors the
shape. An element with its own `::before` keeps the first-line edge only.

**The stem (35.9).** The face's vertical stem is read from the coverage of one row through an "l"
drawn at 200px, and set as `--ink-stem` in em. The stylesheet applies it to the glyph's children,
because any author rule outranks an SVG `stroke-width` attribute, and `non-scaling-stroke` keeps it
true at any size.

## What the engines allow

Blink paints text on the device pixel, so ink lands within three quarters of a CSS pixel of where
Ink puts it. That is half a pixel plus the measurement's own quarter. WebKit and Gecko paint every
baseline on a whole CSS pixel, with or without Ink, so their floor is a pixel and a quarter. The
test's control renders the same cells without Ink: at least 90% of them show more than 2px of extra
air above the capitals, in every engine.

## Mutation record

Each rule was broken on purpose, and the matching requirement failed:

- No trim: 35.1, 35.2, 35.4 and 35.11 fail.
- Inline media: 35.6 fails.
- No float: 35.8 fails.
- No hang: 35.8 fails.
- No size rule: 35.7 fails.
- No stem rule: 35.9 fails.
- No `line-height: 0` on code: 35.3 fails.
- Lists trimmed item by item: 35.13 fails.
